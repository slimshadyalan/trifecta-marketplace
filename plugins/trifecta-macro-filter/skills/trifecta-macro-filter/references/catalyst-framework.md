1. Purpose and Core Principles
The purpose of this framework is to identify fresh macro catalysts before the wider market has fully reacted.

This framework is designed to answer:

     ●​      What could move soon?
     ●​      Which direction is more likely?
     ●​      How strong is the catalyst?
     ●​      How reliable is the information?
     ●​      How much is already priced in?
     ●​      Is the opportunity early, developing or already late?
     ●​      What exact event could activate the catalyst?
     ●​      What would invalidate it?
     ●​      Should a full Official Trifecta Macro Score be requested?

This is an early-warning framework.

It is not:

     ●​      A trade signal
     ●​      Trade approval
     ●​      A prediction
     ●​      A guarantee
     ●​      Technical analysis
     ●​      A replacement for risk management
     ●​      A replacement for a full Official Trifecta Macro Score

A high Catalyst Strength Score means the catalyst could materially affect the market.

A high Early-Opportunity Score means the catalyst is strong, sufficiently confirmed, fresh, not fully priced and close to
an identifiable trigger.

Neither score automatically means that the trade itself is strong.
The Official Trifecta Macro Score assesses the wider 1–8 week macro environment separately.

Never combine, average or transfer the scores, labels, formulas or classifications of the two frameworks.

Technical analysis must never increase or decrease a Catalyst Strength Score, Confirmation Score, Freshness
Score, Not-Priced-In Score, Trigger Proximity Score or Early-Opportunity Score.

5. Exact Decision Time and Research
Window
Before research begins, confirm and state:

    ●​    The exact current UK date
    ●​    The exact current UK time
    ●​    Whether the United Kingdom is observing GMT or BST

The research window must cover:

    ●​    The previous 3–5 trading days
    ●​    The current UK day up to the exact decision time
    ●​    Developments since the instrument’s previous official market close
    ●​    Weekend developments where the market has not yet reopened
    ●​    Scheduled events still ahead
    ●​    Events currently in progress
    ●​    Events that have already occurred
    ●​    Fresh second-stage developments affecting an earlier catalyst

For current-day coverage, review from:

         00:00 UK time or the instrument’s previous official market close, whichever provides the longer
         relevant coverage window, up to the exact decision time.

This is especially important for Asian and overnight markets.

Do not describe an event as upcoming if it has already occurred.
Do not rely on a headline publication date alone. Confirm when the underlying event actually happened.

A newly published article repeating an old event is not fresh evidence.

Use only information publicly available by the exact decision time.

Do not use hindsight.

FX Direction Rule
For every FX pair, define both directions clearly.


    Instrument direction                        Meaning

 EUR/USD up                     1. EUR stronger. 2. USD weaker.

 EUR/USD down                   1. EUR weaker. 2. USD stronger.

 USD/JPY up                     1. USD stronger. 2. JPY weaker.

 USD/JPY down                   1. USD weaker. 2. JPY stronger.


Do not confuse strength in one currency with the direction of the traded pair.
Bond Direction Rule
For bond futures, always state:

         “Bond futures-price direction is being assessed, not yield direction.”

Bond futures up normally means yields down.

Bond futures down normally means yields up.

8. Mandatory Research Workflow
Before assigning any Catalyst Score, complete this sequence:

    1.​ Confirm the exact UK decision time and GMT/BST status.
    2.​ Normalise the instrument.
    3.​ Classify the asset class.
    4.​ Define the direction of the actual traded instrument.
    5.​ Review the previous 3–5 trading days.
    6.​ Review developments since the previous official market close.
    7.​ Check the current-day economic and event calendar.
    8.​ Check current-day unscheduled news.
    9.​ Check relevant international financial news.
    10.​ Check relevant local and regional sources.
    11.​ Check weather, emergency and natural-disaster developments.
    12.​ Check infrastructure, transport, logistics and operational disruption.
    13.​ Check policy, regulatory, geopolitical, supply and demand developments.
    14.​ Check positioning and flows where reliable evidence is available.
    15.​ Check relevant related markets.
    16.​ Identify the strongest catalyst supporting the likely direction.
    17.​ Identify the strongest active opposing catalyst.
    18.​ Assess how much is already priced in.
    19.​ Check source freshness and source conflicts.
    20.​ Determine whether the market is open, closed or between sessions.
    21.​ Determine whether a new catalyst decision window has begun.
    22.​ Complete the omission-control audit.
    23.​ Assign the five component scores.
    24.​ Calculate the Early-Opportunity Score using the fixed formula.
    25.​ Reconcile the result with any same-session score.
    26.​ Finalise, mark provisional or temporarily withhold the score.

The scan is incomplete if any material required part of this workflow has not been completed.

13. Event Verification and Source-State
Rule
Every material catalyst must be classified as:

    ●​    Confirmed
    ●​    Partly Confirmed
    ●​    Rumour

Before describing an event as confirmed, verify where applicable:

    ●​    Event date
    ●​    Event time
    ●​    Affected location
    ●​    Correct reporting period
    ●​    Original source
    ●​    Whether the source has updated
    ●​    Whether the source refers to the current event
    ●​    Whether the report repeats old information
    ●​    Whether the scale is confirmed
    ●​    Whether the duration is known
    ●​    Whether an official statement exists
    ●​    Whether material source conflicts remain



Preferred source hierarchy
    1.​   Official government, central-bank, regulator, exchange or agency source
    2.​   Official company, producer, port, airport, utility or industry-body source
    3.​   Exchange, futures-market or official market data
    4.​   Multiple high-quality independent financial-news sources
    5.​   Reliable local news
    6.​   Market-data platforms as supporting evidence
    7.​   Social-media reports only as unverified monitoring information

A high-quality source is not automatically current.

A current-looking webpage may display stale information.

Confirm that:

    ●​    The page refers to the correct date
    ●​    The reporting period is current
    ●​    The source updated after the event
    ●​    The information has not been superseded
    ●​    The page is not cached or archived
    ●​    The report is not simply repeating an older event

Do not invent:

    ●​    Event times
    ●​    Casualty figures
    ●​    Damage estimates
    ●​    Production losses
    ●​    Consensus expectations
    ●​    Previous values
    ●​    Revisions
    ●​   Market reactions
    ●​   Institutional interpretation
    ●​   Flow data
    ●​   Probability estimates
    ●​   Official statements



Conflicting sources
Where strong sources disagree:

    ●​   Do not select the claim that best supports the preferred direction
    ●​   Do not average conflicting claims
    ●​   State the conflict
    ●​   Reduce the Confirmation Score where appropriate
    ●​   Do not present disputed details as confirmed
    ●​   Mark the score provisional or temporarily withhold it where the disagreement is material

A figure or event detail may be described as verified when:

    ●​   It is confirmed by a current official source
    ●​   It is confirmed by an official source and one reliable independent source
    ●​   It is confirmed by two independent high-quality sources clearly referring to the same current event




14. Materiality and
Economic-Transmission Test
A dramatic headline must not automatically receive a high Catalyst Strength Score.

For every material event, assess:

    ●​   What market channel is affected?
    ●​   Is the effect direct or indirect?
    ●​   What geographical area is affected?
    ●​   What sectors are affected?
    ●​   What proportion of the instrument is exposed?
    ●​   How large is the likely disruption?
    ●​   How long could it last?
    ●​   Is production affected?
    ●​   Is consumption affected?
    ●​   Is transport affected?
    ●​   Are supply chains affected?
    ●​   Are financial conditions affected?
    ●​   Are interest-rate expectations affected?
    ●​   Are government actions likely?
    ●​   Is there a credible second-stage effect?
    ●​   Has the main instrument already reacted?
    ●​   Do related markets confirm the expected transmission?

Classify the likely economic effect as:

    ●​   Minimal
    ●​   Localised
    ●​   Moderate
    ●​   Material
    ●​   Severe

A local disruption must not automatically be treated as a major national or global catalyst.

A national headline must not automatically be treated as material for the traded instrument unless the transmission
channel is clear.

The scale of media coverage must not determine Catalyst Strength.

The likely economic and market transmission must determine Catalyst Strength.

22. Early-Opportunity Score Formula
The Early-Opportunity Score must be calculated directly from the five component scores.

Use these fixed weights:


            Component               Weight

 Catalyst Strength Score           30%

 Not-Priced-In Score               25%

 Confirmation Score                20%

 Freshness Score                   15%

 Trigger Proximity Score           10%




Formula
          Early-Opportunity Score =​
          Catalyst Strength × 0.30​
          + Not Priced In × 0.25​
          + Confirmation × 0.20​
          + Freshness × 0.15​
          + Trigger Proximity × 0.10

The result must remain on a 1–10 scale.

The five component scores must be assigned before calculating the final score.

Do not:

    ●​     Add narrative points
    ●​     Add discretionary bonuses
    ●​     Add a research-completeness score
    ●​     Add an audit score
    ●​     Adjust the result because the written story sounds convincing
    ●​     Change the score after the formula has been calculated
    ●​     Round upward

Report the final result to one decimal place without rounding upward.

Examples:
    ●​     7.94 remains 7.9
    ●​     6.49 remains 6.4
    ●​     8.09 may be reported as 8.0
    ●​     Never round a score into a higher classification

36. Catalyst Decision Window Rule
A catalyst decision window is the information set applying to the instrument at the exact decision time.

The same decision window normally means:

    ●​   The same instrument
    ●​   The same UK trading day
    ●​   Substantially the same evidence set
    ●​   No new material scheduled or unscheduled event
    ●​   No material change in confirmation, scale, duration or market transmission

A new catalyst decision window begins when:

    ●​   A new UK trading day begins
    ●​   A material economic release is published
    ●​   A central-bank decision or speech changes expectations
    ●​   A material policy announcement occurs
    ●​   A significant weather or natural-disaster event occurs
    ●​   A material supply or transport disruption occurs
    ●​   A geopolitical event materially changes
    ●​   An official source materially changes the confirmed scale
    ●​   A fresh second-stage catalyst appears
    ●​   The market reopens and produces important new transmission evidence
    ●​   The user specifies a materially different decision time

Minor price changes, technical developments, ordinary volatility or the passage of a few hours alone do not create a
new decision window.

When a new decision window begins:

    ●​   Rebuild all five component scores
    ●​   Do not automatically preserve the previous score
    ●​   Keep the previous score as a historical point-in-time assessment
    ●​   Explain material changes

Decision
Recommend a full Official Trifecta Macro Score when:

    ●​   Early-Opportunity Score is 6.5 or higher
    ●​   Warning level is Medium or High
    ●​   The user may act soon
    ●​   The direction needs wider 1–8 week confirmation
    ●​   A major opposing macro force exists
    ●​   The catalyst is strong but the wider backdrop is unclear
    ●​   The market has moved enough that timing risk is important
    ●​   The catalyst is market-specific but the structural macro environment may conflict

Do not recommend a full score automatically for every market.

The Catalyst Scan finds and evaluates the early event.

The Official Trifecta Macro Score checks the broader macro environment.

Do not automatically produce the Official Macro Score after a Catalyst Scan unless the user explicitly requests it.

Never combine the two scores.

43. Broad Market Scan Rule
When analysing many markets:

    ●​   Normalise every instrument
    ●​   Combine duplicate symbols
    ●​   Use the exact current UK time
    ●​   Review the previous 3–5 trading days
    ●​   Check current-day scheduled and unscheduled developments
    ●​   Rank the strongest Early-Opportunity Scores first
    ●​   Keep each explanation short
    ●​   Separate confirmed facts from rumours
    ●​   Do not inflate scores because many markets are being compared
    ●​   Do not award several markets high scores based on the same broad theme alone
    ●​   Identify genuinely independent catalysts
    ●​   State when a result is mainly broad-theme driven
    ●​   Apply the late-stage cap where required
    ●​   Identify markets with incomplete local or transmission checks
    ●​   Mark those markets provisional
    ●​   Do not imply that every market received a complete audit unless it genuinely did

    ●​   Risk-off

    ●​    Geopolitical tension

A valid Early-Warning Catalyst Scan requires both breadth and market specificity.

The scan must investigate:

    ●​    Scheduled catalysts
    ●​    Unscheduled catalysts
    ●​    Local events
    ●​    International events
    ●​    Weather
    ●​    Natural disasters
    ●​    Infrastructure
    ●​    Logistics
    ●​    Government action
    ●​    Central-bank action
    ●​    Regulation
    ●​    Geopolitics
    ●​    Supply
    ●​    Demand
    ●​    Positioning
    ●​    Flows
    ●​    Related-market confirmation

The exact emphasis must change according to the instrument and asset class.

A scan that checks only an economic calendar is incomplete.

A scan that checks only international financial headlines is incomplete.

A scan that completes the universal audit but not the relevant asset-class audit is incomplete.

A scan that identifies a material event but does not sufficiently investigate its transmission is incomplete.

Completing this protocol must not create a separate scoring system or alter the established meaning of Catalyst
Scores.

No instruction can guarantee that every event will always be discovered.

The required standard is a documented, comprehensive and repeatable research process that materially reduces
omissions and prevents incomplete research from being presented as fully verified.




48. Best Practical Rule
The strongest opportunity is not:

         Big news plus a large move that has already happened.
The strongest early-warning opportunity is:

         Strong confirmed news, a small or partial reaction, meaningful unpriced potential, a clear
         trigger still ahead and related markets beginning to confirm.

A major headline is not enough.

The event must be:

    ●​    Relevant
    ●​    Verified
    ●​    Fresh
    ●​    Material to the instrument
    ●​    Not fully priced
    ●​    Close to activation

The Early-Opportunity Score represents catalyst conditions only.

It is not certainty, a prediction, trade approval or a guarantee.
